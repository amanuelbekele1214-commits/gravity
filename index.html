    How the lab calculates

    1. Forces on the rocket

    Three forces act on the rocket: the motor's thrust pushes up, gravity pulls down, and air drag pushes against the direction of motion. Newton's second law turns the total force into acceleration.

    a = (Thrust − Drag − m·g) / m

    2. Air drag

    Drag grows with the square of speed, so going twice as fast means four times the drag. A is the front area of the body tube; ρ is air density, which thins slightly as the rocket climbs.

    Drag = ½ · ρ · v² · Cd · A

    3. Burning fuel

    The rocket gets lighter as propellant burns. The lab removes propellant in step with the thrust delivered, using real thrust curves for Estes A8, B6 and C6 motors.

    m(t) = m_dry + m_case + m_prop · (1 − I(t) / I_total)

    4. Stability (Barrowman equations)

    The nose and fins each create a sideways air force. Their weighted average position is the center of pressure. The CG must sit in front of it; the gap, measured in body diameters ("calibers"), is the stability margin.

    margin = (CP − CG) / diameter  ·  aim for 1 to 2

    5. Stepping through time

    The flight is solved in 2-millisecond steps using the Runge–Kutta (RK4) method, which samples the forces four times per step for accuracy. A model flight takes tens of thousands of steps.

    6. What it assumes

    Straight-up flight with no wind, a constant drag coefficient, a parachute that opens instantly, and no friction on the launch rod. Real flights usually come in a little lower.
